Accounts: one users table linked to the school's records

  1. students and employees are the school's records: who exists in the school. A record exists whether or not the person has registered. users is who has an account: every Student, Faculty / Employee, staff, administrator and master administrator login is one row, so sessions, the security log and every action point to users.user_id.
  2. Registration: the person enters their student or employee number. The server checks the format, looks it up in students or employees, checks that the record may register and that no account already links to it, then creates the users row with the number in users.student_number or users.employee_number. The browser never chooses the link or the role (always user).
  3. The links are unique foreign keys (one account per school record, a record with no account is simply unregistered; there is no separate “registered” flag) with ON DELETE RESTRICT, so deleting a school record can never delete an account or its history. A check allows at most one of the two links.
  4. Names are not copied. A linked account's name is read from its school record (USERS_SQL in src/repository.php); users.first_name / last_name hold a name only for accounts with no school record (seeded office accounts, and Student / Faculty accounts registered before school records existed that no record matches). A check enforces this. The birth date is read from the record to reject passwords that contain it.
  5. Role and identity are separate: role says what an account may do (user for students and faculty, who have the same rights; staff, admin, master_admin), and the link says who it is. A staff or administrator account may link to its employee record.
  6. Staff and administrator accounts are seeded (migrations, with SEED_PASSWORD replacing the sample password on deploy) or provisioned by an administrator changing an existing account's role. They never come from the registration form.

Check constraints:

  • users.chk_users_one_school_id: ((student_number is null) or (employee_number is null))
  • users.chk_users_name_source: (((student_number is null) and (employee_number is null)) = ((first_name is not null) and (last_name is not null)))

Status model (no duplicate status systems)

  • claims.status is the main claim status: pending → approved (approved for collection) → verified (identity checked at the desk) → returned. A rejection does not change it: the claim stays pending.
  • The current review outcome is the latest claim_reviews row (approved / rejected); there is no separate outcome column that could disagree with the history. Every decision is a new row with its reviewer, reason, previous outcome and time.
  • found_items.status keeps its three values; returned and disposed are the two final outcomes. The return record is on the item (returned_at, returned_by, return_claim_id, unique), the disposal record too (disposed_at, disposed_by, disposal_reason, disposal_notes): no separate returned-items or disposed-items table.
  • claim_verifications stores one row per identity check: ID type, the last four characters of the ID number only (cleared after the retention period), result, the validating staff member and the database time.
